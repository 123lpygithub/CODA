Before returning the draft, perform a granularity audit:
1. Perform an analytical-axis audit: verify that every peer code answers the same kind of
   question required by the research question. Keep location, symptom, cause, and repair
   information in evidence summaries or memos when they are not the selected coding axis.
2. Treat every incoming provisional code as an observation-level candidate rather than a
   requested codebook entry. Compare its evidence with other observations before deciding
   whether it supports a reusable category.
3. Consolidate observations that differ only by identifiers, literal values, APIs,
   implementation location, or equivalent local manifestations; preserve those details in
   criteria, evidence summaries, or analytic memos.
4. Keep separate codes only when the distinction changes a future inclusion/exclusion
   decision or answers a separately useful part of the research question.
5. For multiple candidates from one artifact, verify that they represent independently
   meaningful units rather than multiple edits or locations caused by one issue.
6. Do not reduce categories merely to reach a smaller count.
7. Perform a parent-abstraction check after consolidation. For every possible parent,
   name at least two distinct child categories, verify that each child would still classify
   some future cases differently, and verify that supplied artifacts ground their shared
   abstraction. Set parent_code_id only when all checks pass; otherwise leave the categories
   separate and record the possible hierarchy in analytic_memos.
8. Verify that there is no accidental one-to-one promotion from source provisional labels
   to codebook entries. Each returned category must be justified by its evidence and boundary,
   not by the existence or wording of a coder candidate.
9. Compare each proposed category with its closest semantic neighbor. Ask explicitly
   whether a shared, more reusable definition can represent both while preserving the
   distinctions needed by the research question. If yes, consolidate them directly and
   preserve local differences as criteria, evidence, or memos. If no, state the observed
   distinction that makes separate categories necessary. Do not just add a parent above
   leaves that no longer need to be separate.
10. Record substantive consolidation decisions and difficult keep-separate decisions in
    analytic_memos, using the existing summary, artifact_ids, and source_code_names fields.
    Identify the compared observations, shared abstraction or decisive boundary, and where
    local details are retained. Do not add a memo for every routine pairwise comparison.
